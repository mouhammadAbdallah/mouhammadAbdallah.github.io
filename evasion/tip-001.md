# [Evasion tip 1/100] - Make sure your loader entropy resembles legitimate PE entropies

Bit entropy measures how "random" the bytes in a PE look. In a legitimate binary, sections like `.text` and `.rdata` often land in a mid range: structured code and data, not pure noise. Packing, encryption, or heavy compression push entropy toward the ceiling (closer to 8.0). Analysts and EDR heuristics use that spike as a cheap signal that something may be packed or obfuscated.

Across large corpora, malware tends to dominate above ~7.0, while another large cluster of samples sits around ~4.4. That split is why a loader that sits in the high-entropy band stands out next to normal Windows PE files.

So for tooling artifacts, the least suspicious window is roughly **4.8-6.8**: high enough to look like real code/data, low enough to avoid the "almost random blob" bucket.

## Ways to land there

- Prefer light or selective packing over full-file encryption when you can
- Mix structured padding or low-entropy regions (zeros, repeated patterns, embedded resource-like data) with the high-entropy core so the *file-wide* average drops
- Align section layout and entropy profile with common compiler/linker output instead of one opaque high-entropy blob
- Measure per-section and whole-file entropy on every build and treat 4.8-6.8 as a release gate

Entropy alone does not mean "malicious" or "clean." It is one cheap feature among many. Matching legitimate PE ranges just removes an easy early filter.
